# Practice mock interviews (Horizon3 scenarios)

**Master list:** [notes/master-list.md](master-list.md)

## Idea
This month's focus: practice mock interviews. Specifically want to break down and practice scenarios from the last interview with Horizon3's hiring manager.

## Translation
You have a real, recent interview to learn from instead of generic prep — the plan is to pull apart what actually happened with Horizon3's hiring manager and turn it into practice scenarios, so next time you're sharper on that exact kind of conversation.

## Questions to Practice
1. **Fast onboarding + parallel execution** — New domain, startup speed: how do you ramp up product expertise *and* ship small wins at the same time? What's your technique?
2. **Alignment workshop** — High-visibility project, small tiger team (design/eng/PM), CEO watching: what do you run early (workshops, etc.) to get shared understanding fast and avoid design rework later?
3. **Handling engineering pushback** — Design is locked, ready to hand off — engineering comes back saying it's too ambitious for the timeline they committed to. How do you react and resolve it?
4. **AI in the design process** — Real experience prototyping with AI tools (Claude Code, etc.) or building skills to speed up design. How does it reshape the traditional design process?
5. **Career arc** — A few years out: individual-contributor craft, or design leadership? What does the ideal future look like?

## Stories to Build
- **Zero-to-one ownership/vision story** — the exact gap the hiring manager named. This is the core one everything else orbits.
- **Workshop + stakeholder-alignment story** — CEO, PM, eng, cross-team.
- **THOUGHT::DESIGN trio, as one lived story** — ownership without authority, design→business influence, startup alignment. Same Horizon3 gap, restated three ways — proof you've actually lived it, not just theory.

## Unanswered Pieces (from coaching feedback)
- **What's on the wall after the 90-minute room?** Don't just imply the activity — name it and commit: sketch together (Crazy-8s)? map + rank assumptions by risk? "How Might We"? user journey map? Pick the one you'd actually run, and say what the room produces by the time people walk out.
- **How do you prevent UX iteration — say the mechanism out loud.** It's implied in a wireframes-then-validate flow, but needs to be stated directly: kill expensive rework by agreeing on the problem + what success looks like before anyone touches a pixel, stay lo-fi until aligned, test a rough prototype with real users before going hi-fi. Confirm this is really how you think about it, or state it differently in your own words.

## Outcomes (what "ready" looks like)
- Fully confident in your design process when things are urgent or fast-onboarding.
- Confident answering all 5 Horizon3 scenarios.
- Hiring-manager themes covered: workshop-to-alignment, day-one readiness, onboarding under pressure.
